# Deep analysis of the 5 reference shorts

Source: 5 vertical shorts (576×1024, 30 fps, 15.7–27.9 s) from one creator (watermark on the
last clip). Same format every time: an absurd mini-story told by a deadpan narrator, with a
low-poly 3D businessman, burned-in captions and a hard cut roughly every 1–2 s.
Timings below come from the burned-in captions, sampled at 2.5 fps (±0.2 s).

> **format, rhythm and comedic mechanics**. Everything we generate (scripts, character

## 3. Joke mechanics (why it works)
1. **Literal causality.** Each sentence follows *mechanically* from the one before (the blower blows → the eyebrows fly → they land on a pigeon). Nothing is random, yet everything is absurd.
2. **Disproportionate institutions.** Police, SWAT, court, judge, fines, "legal fees", prison show up in 4 of 5 videos. Bureaucracy treats a ridiculous event with total seriousness.
3. **Personifying the inanimate or animal.** The tornado gets handcuffs and is tried; the milk is interrogated; the lion is sued; the ant sells a human. Rights and duties go to things that can't have them.
4. **Role / scale reversal.** The human becomes a pet ($5), the plaintiff goes to prison, the predator ends up homeless, the man becomes the pigeon's passenger.
5. **Legal vocabulary as punchline.** "That was trespassing." "That was a break-in." Two words, delivered flat, that turn a disaster into an offence.
6. **The dry tag.** The last sentence adds a petty economic or everyday detail ("The glass cost extra.", "and lives on the street."). Downbeat and terse, with no "and that's how…".
7. **Deadpan contrast.** The voice never laughs. The character barely reacts: a blank stare at the camera after every absurd beat.
8. **Swearing as the button.** One big swear word, saved for the climax ("Who the f*** keeps turning off the light?", "The whole goddamn time."), lands as the release.
9. **Connectives "So…" / "Then…".** "So he…" appears in 4/5 videos, "Then…" in 4/5. They hold the causal chain together: *Donc / Alors* and *Puis / Ensuite* in French.

### Canonical template (8 beats)
```
B1 HOOK        This man [verb in the past] [ordinary object] to [ordinary goal].    (0–3 s)
B2 RESULT      It worked. / immediate physical consequence.                         (short)
B3 TURN        Then [an element] [does something unexpected].
B4 QUALIFY     That was [legal term].  (optional; or an absurd "logical" observation)
B5 INSTITUTION So he called the police / sued [X].
B6 ESCALATION  disproportionate response: SWAT, helicopter, "their two best men", judge…
B7 TWIST       role reversal / reveal (the man is convicted, sold, flown away; the guilty party revealed)
B8 TAG         dry everyday detail. Silent hold.
```

## 5. Animation (observed)
- A rigid **puppet** rig: every limb is a box, no skin deformation. Movement is snappy with few in-betweens.
- **Idle**: tiny breathing, almost motionless. **Holds** last 0.5–1.5 s on the face after each joke.
- Key poses: hands on hips (repeatedly), one hand on hip + one arm raised holding a prop, salute/hand at the temple, hand reaching out to grab, high-knee cartoon sprint with bent arms, seated at a desk, **slumped head-down on the desk** (defeat ending), stiff as a board hanging off a wire, arms pressed against glass.
- Objects scale up smoothly (sandwich, ant), the tornado spins on stacked rings, wings flap fast, the helicopter rotor turns, and the officers come down the ladder in an endless stream.

**Rule**: one take per location. The camera *glides* from beat to beat (push, pan, tilt, follow) in a continuous move.
Cut only when the location changes (or for the fast V5 style). Motion per take (mean frame diff, 144×256) = 5–15:
something is always moving (an object being sucked up, an animal growing, the camera following).
In the engine: `"cut": false` on a shot = no cut (set/actors/props carried over at their end state, the camera glides from
the previous framing, `camera.glide` = glide duration).

### Previous (reference) notes

- 9:16 framing, slightly low angle, **3/4 view** (yaw ≈ 15–25°) for medium shots; faces in **frontal ECU** for reactions.
- **Slow push-in** on nearly every shot (5–15% over the shot). A side tracking shot for chases. Low angles for threats (ant, helicopter). A white flash for the speed camera.
- Hard cuts in sync with the captions. One shot per sentence, sometimes two.
- Grade: soft palette with a light vignette and paper grain. Cream / teal / navy / brick red, plus a night variant in blue-black.

## 7. Captions
- Bold sans-serif (Arial/Helvetica Black weight), white with a fine dark outline and soft shadow, **centred at ~65–80% of the height**.
- **Highlighted words in salmon/red #E8775F**: the nouns and adjectives that carry the joke.
- 1–4 words per chunk, cut at punctuation, sentence-ending punctuation kept on screen.

## 8. Sound
- Deep male narrator (median F0 ≈ 110 Hz, p10 85 Hz), totally **flat**, fast, with almost no pauses (0.25–0.4 s between sentences).
- A constant soft music bed around −40 dBFS (the noise floor never drops to silence) and very few sound effects.
- The dialogue line (V5) is delivered by another voice and caught in quotation marks in the captions.
