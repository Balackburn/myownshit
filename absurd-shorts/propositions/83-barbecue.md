# Histoire 83 — « Le barbecue »

Écrite par la boucle d'écriture : cinq nouveaux auteurs (police, armée, justice, secours et État, escalade totale), plus « Le détecteur », ex æquo de l'histoire 82, remis en compétition. Jury de trois (comédie, rétention, fabrication), puis réécriture par l'auteur principal. Interdits pour cet épisode, parce que « La guêpe » et « La flaque » les ont utilisés : sous-marin, torpille, porte-avions, frappe aérienne qui multiplie le problème, « trois cents X », ville déplacée, cessez-le-feu, médailles, rasage « par précaution », char à travers une façade, héros condamné, coupable qui était une autre chose, dernier plan où le héros refait la même erreur.

## Texte final

1. Ce mec était enfumé par le barbecue du voisin.  — *Medium shot with the hero in the foreground. Over a low garden fence, the neighbour's guests laugh and clink glasses around a tiny smoking grill, while the hero stands alone on his side and a thick grey ribbon of smoke flows over the fence straight into his box face.*
2. Il a crié à l'attaque chimique.  — *The hero, dead serious, on the phone, with a curl of smoke slipping into the receiver. Behind him, over the fence, the party carries on.*
3. Le président a déclenché l'alerte nucléaire.  — *The president, in his sash, brings his fist down on a big red button that squashes flat, and red sirens start spinning on the wall behind him.*
4. Tout le pays a mis un masque à gaz.  — *Wide street shot: a whole crowd of box-headed citizens stands frozen in gas masks. Every head turns together to follow a single wisp of smoke drifting past.*
5. L'armée a parachuté tout un régiment.  — *Low angle from the little garden: the sky fills with dozens of parachutes floating down onto the tiny grill, while the guests look up with sausages still on their forks.*
6. Un seul a atterri sur le gril.  — *One paratrooper lands bottom-first on the tiny grill, smoke rising from his trousers, and holds a rigid salute without moving.*
7. Les autres, chez ce mec.  — *The hero's own house, its roof caved in under a heap of white parachute canopies, with a pair of boots kicking out of the chimney. The hero stands in front of it in his gas mask.*
8. Six saucisses ont été faites prisonnières.  — *Six sausages stand upright in a row inside the existing prison cell, and a soldier slams the barred door on them. CAPTURÉES stamps.*
9. Une chipolata a refusé de parler.  — *Interrogation room: a single chipolata sits on a chair under a hanging lamp. An officer slams the table, and the chipolata hops up and lands back down, silent.*
10. Elle a été échangée contre un espion.  — *On a foggy bridge, two soldiers carry the chipolata one way on a tiny stretcher, while a spy in a trench coat walks past them the other way.*
11. L'armée a éteint le barbecue au lance-flammes.  — *A soldier in a gas mask blasts the tiny grill with a huge roaring jet of flame from a flamethrower.*
12. Le quartier a pris feu.  — *Wide shot: the whole neighbourhood is in flames behind the soldier, and the grill is a small black lump in the foreground. ÉTEINT stamps over the picture.*
13. Le parachutiste n'a pas bougé.  — *Close on the paratrooper from shot 6, still sitting on the charred black grill and still holding his rigid salute, while the whole neighbourhood burns behind him; smoke rises from his trousers and his face is smudged with charcoal-grey soot patches (not a uniform colour). He does not blink.*
14. Pour rassurer, le ministre a mangé une saucisse.  — *Press room: the minister at his podium bites into a sausage on a fork under a storm of camera flashes.*
15. Il a été placé en quarantaine.  — *A transparent plastic bubble drops over the minister mid-bite, cheeks full, with the flashes still popping. QUARANTAINE stamps.*
16. Le président a décrété un barbecue national.  — *The president, in his sash, raises a sausage on a fork like a toast in front of a giant smoking grill, while the military band plays and invented flags wave over a cheering crowd.*
17. Sur les braises du quartier.  — *Soldiers and citizens crouch in a row roasting sausages on sticks over the glowing ruins of the neighbourhood; in the middle the paratrooper still sits saluting on the black grill, a sausage now held out to him on a stick.*
18. Ah, et il n'était pas invité.  — *Same framing as shot 1: the hero stands alone behind the charred stump of his fence in his gas mask, holding an empty paper plate, while the national barbecue's smoke drifts into his mask. In the background the neighbour clinks glasses with a general.*

Cartons : CAPTURÉES — ligne 8 (Six saucisses ont été faites prisonnières.) ; ÉTEINT — ligne 12 (Le quartier a pris feu.) ; QUARANTAINE — ligne 15 (Il a été placé en quarantaine.)

## Retouches après la réécriture (éditeur)

- Ligne 2 : « Il a signalé une attaque chimique. » devient « Il a crié à l'attaque chimique. ». « La flaque » venait d'utiliser la même tournure, « Il a signalé une inondation. », et « crier à » dit en plus qu'il exagère.
- Ligne 13 : « La caserne des pompiers en premier. » est remplacée par « Le parachutiste n'a pas bougé. ». « La flaque » venait de détruire une caserne de pompiers (« Et la caserne des pompiers. ») : on ne reprend pas un gag à l'identique. Le parachutiste resté au garde-à-vous sur le gril pendant que le quartier brûle reprend la ligne 6 et devient un fil rouge.
- Ligne 17 : « Sur les braises de la caserne. » devient « Sur les braises du quartier. », avec le parachutiste toujours assis au milieu, à qui l'on tend une saucisse.

## Changements apportés par la réécriture

- Hook: replaced the clumsy 'Ce mec respirait le barbecue du voisin.' with 'Ce mec était enfumé par le barbecue du voisin.' (9 words). The panel's version, 'se prenait la fumée du barbecue du voisin', counts 10 words, which breaks the 9-word limit. The new line is natural French and keeps both 'barbecue' and 'voisin', so the grievance reads in two seconds.
- Planted the twist without narration: shot 1 now shows the neighbour's guests laughing and clinking glasses while the hero stands alone. The last line became the offhand 'Ah, et il n'était pas invité.' (six words), the driest option. It reframes the whole war as the hero's pettiness. Shot 18 copies shot 1's framing so the payoff also reads with the sound off.
- Line 3: the nuclear alert is now the president's decision ('Le président a déclenché l'alerte nucléaire.'), so the jump from chemical to nuclear reads as a choice, not a mistake. It also bookends with the president's decree in line 16.
- Cut 'Deux cents parachutistes', which was close to the banned multiplying count, and replaced it with 'L'armée a parachuté tout un régiment.' This fixes flat line 5: the line is now an overreaction and it sets up the next two lines.
- Added a hero-pays beat after the grill landing: 'Les autres, chez ce mec.' It grafts candidate 1's 'Chez ce mec.' logic over his own house caved in under parachutes. The beat fits this story's logic and gives one more quotable line.
- Line 8 (sausage prisoners): moved from the barbed-wire camp with a watchtower to the prison cell model we already have. It reads faster on a phone and removes the real-war echo.
- Moved ÉTEINT from the flamethrower shot to the wide shot of the burning neighbourhood (line 12), so the stamp contradicts the picture. CAPTURÉES and QUARANTAINE stay on their beats, now lines 8 and 15.
- Kept 'La caserne des pompiers en premier.' word for word. No 'par précaution' or 'Et la mairie' wording anywhere, so the collateral beat stays clear of « La guêpe ».
- Quarantine: the cut lands on the gag. The bubble drops mid-bite with cheeks full and flashes still firing. Line 14 stays at 8 words so the pair plays in about four seconds.
- Replaced flat line 15, 'L'armée a fêté sa victoire au barbecue.', with 'Le président a décrété un barbecue national.' This is new machinery (a national decree, close to a national holiday). The State now imposes the very thing it was called to stop, and it sharpens the last line: the whole country is invited except him.
- Line 16 → 17: 'Sur les braises du quartier' became 'Sur les braises de la caserne.' It now calls back the burning fire station, with the firemen still holding their limp hose. That makes it a new collateral-as-detail image rather than a second celebration shot.
- Removed anything that could read as medals or decorations from the staging (banned). The victory shows only through the band, the invented flags and the grill.
- Rules check: 18 lines, all 4 to 9 words, last line 6 words, French only, no real names, numbers in words ('Six'), line 1 starts with « Ce mec », no exclamation marks. Estimated spoken length is about 33 to 34 seconds.

## Second : « La bougie »

A man can't blow out his birthday candle, so the emergency services and the State throw their whole crisis plan at it, and wreck a district doing it, until the State makes it the eternal flame and it goes out the next morning.

## Autres candidats

- Le ballon (police) : A man's ball lands over the hedge and the neighbour keeps it, so he reports a hostage-taking; the police run the full hostage procedure, raid his house, shoot the ball, look after the neighbour and send the man the bill.
- L'oignon (justice) : A man cries while cutting an onion and sues it for assault; the justice system arrests, tries and jails the onion with total seriousness, the onion becomes the State's best interrogator, and the man gets the bill.
- Le nœud (total) : A man finds his earphones tangled, and the State takes the knot on with everything it has: interrogation, the bomb squad, the army, the courts. It blows up a police station, flattens a ministry, sends a tank abroad and finally jails the knot for life, earphones included.
- « Le détecteur » (retour) : A man's smoke detector beeps for a low battery, so the firemen, finding no fire, light one to stay within protocol. The State's full emergency plan then burns and floods the whole neighbourhood, and the detector is the only thing left untouched, until the bomb squad kills the battery, which gets a state funeral and keeps beeping from the grave.
