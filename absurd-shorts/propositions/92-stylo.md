# Histoire 92 — « Le stylo »

Écrite par trois auteurs en parallèle (science et sport, armée et État, médias et administration), départagée par trois juges (comédie, rétention, fabrication), corrigée selon leurs notes et vérifiée contre tous les épisodes publiés.

Prémisse : A man walks off with the town hall's pen, which turns out to be the only pen of the State; the whole country stops until it is found in his pocket, empty for ten years, and his own new pen is chained at the counter in its place, where he visits it on Thursdays.

## Texte final

1. Ce mec a empoché le stylo de la mairie.  — *Town hall counter, low medium shot from counter height: the hero signs with the State pen (one oversized hero prop used in every pen shot: forearm-length brass ballpoint with a thick chain and a big ÉTAT tag), absent-mindedly snaps the chain and clips it into his shirt pocket next to the hero's own new pen (oversized like the State pen, glossy red, no tag), then walks out; the cut chain swings on the counter.*
2. Le seul stylo de l'État.  — *Close-up of the empty counter: the cut chain with its big ÉTAT tag dangles over a dust outline of the brass pen; the clerk stares, frozen. Slow push-in.*
3. Les dossiers se sont empilés jusqu'au plafond.  — *Town hall office, low wide angle: towers of unsigned files shoot up from every desk and press against the ceiling; a clerk peeks out between two towers holding the empty chain.*
4. Les mariages ont été suspendus.  — *Town hall wedding: bride and groom before the mayor in his sash; the mayor holds up the empty chain, closes the register and hangs a SUSPENDU sign on it. Frontal medium shot.*
5. Les naissances, reportées à lundi.  — *Hospital corridor: a nurse tapes a big LUNDI sign on the maternity door; a row of expectant fathers sits back down on a bench. Low dolly along the bench.*
6. Édition spéciale. Onzième jour sans stylo.  — *State TV studio: the unshaven anchor under a red ÉDITION SPÉCIALE banner, a giant photo of the brass pen behind him; the on-screen counter flips to ONZE and the paper stack on his desk topples. Camera cranes down onto the desk.*
7. Le président a voulu démissionner.  — *Press room, low angle: the president with his sash at the podium holds a resignation letter up to the cameras; flashes pop.*
8. Impossible : il fallait signer.  — *Same podium, medium shot: a minister holds out the empty chain with nothing on the end; the president lowers the letter, and two ministers slide the sash back over his shoulder.*
9. La douane a fouillé chaque trousse d'écolier.  — *Schoolyard, low wide tracking shot: customs officers in caps and blue gloves empty pencil cases onto a folding table, holding each small pencil up against a life-size cardboard cutout of the brass pen; children wait in line with open schoolbags.*
10. Sa mère l'a dénoncé.  — *Police station counter, medium two-shot: a small box-headed old lady slides a framed photo of the hero across to a policeman; in the photo the brass pen sticks out of his pocket.*
11. Les forces spéciales ont sauté sur son balcon.  — *Night, low angle from the street: a helicopter with searchlight over an apartment block; masked special forces rope down onto the hero's fifth-floor balcony.*
12. Le balcon a cédé. Et les trois du dessous.  — *Same block, wide static shot: the balcony drops and takes the three empty balconies below with it into a neat stack of slabs on the pavement; the commandos sit on top, unhurt, dusty, still aiming.*
13. Le stylo était dans sa poche. Mâchouillé.  — *Hero in pyjamas against the wall: a gloved hand pulls the brass pen from his pocket and holds it up into the searchlight beam; its cap is chewed flat; the red pen stays clipped in the pocket. Close-up.*
14. Il n'avait plus d'encre depuis dix ans.  — *Forensic table, top-down shot: a technician in a white suit scribbles the brass pen across a big white board; nothing appears; she shakes it hard, scribbles again, nothing.*
15. Son stylo neuf est enchaîné au guichet.  — *Back at the town hall counter, counter height: the hero's red pen now hangs on the thick chain with the ÉTAT tag; the clerk signs a wedding register with it while a queue forms; the hero watches from the door, his pocket empty.*
16. Ce mec a parloir le jeudi.  — *Prison visiting booth, frontal static shot: the hero sits on the visitor side of the glass holding the phone; on the other side his red pen lies on a little stool on its chain, a guard standing beside it; a wall calendar behind reads JEUDI.*

Cartes : REPORTÉ (line 5), SAISI (line 13), ENCHAÎNÉ (line 15).

Gag récurrent : Every institution needs a signature to act and has nothing to sign with, so each shot shows the same empty chain with its ÉTAT tag: files pile to the ceiling, weddings and births stop, the president cannot even resign. At the end the hero's own pen takes the State pen's place on the chain, and he becomes its visitor.

## Accessoires nouveaux

- oversized brass State pen (forearm length) on a thick chain with a big ÉTAT tag; a chewed-cap variant
- oversized glossy red pen (the hero's own)
- town hall counter (guichet) with the cut chain
- towers of unsigned files reaching the ceiling
- wedding register with a SUSPENDU sign
- maternity door with a LUNDI sign
- pencil cases on a customs folding table, with a cardboard cutout of the pen
- stack of four collapsed balcony slabs
- forensic scribble board
- prison visiting booth with glass, phone and a little stool
