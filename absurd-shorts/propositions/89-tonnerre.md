# Histoire 89 — « Le tonnerre »

Prémisse : Un homme réveillé par le tonnerre porte plainte, et la justice arrête, juge et enferme l'orage avant de l'assigner à résidence chez lui.

## Texte final

1. Ce mec a été réveillé par le tonnerre.  — *Night bedroom: the hero in pyjamas and nightcap jolts upright in bed as the window flashes white; outside, a huge dark anvil-shaped thundercloud with a box face scowls.*
2. Il a porté plainte pour nuisances sonores.  — *Police front desk at night: the hero in pyjamas points up at the window while a policeman types with one finger.*
3. Le tonnerre a été interpellé à trois mille mètres.  — *High above a tiny town: helicopters with searchlights circle the black thundercloud while masked special forces abseil onto it and clamp oversized handcuffs on its little zigzag bolt arms. Card INTERPELLÉ stamps.*
4. Deux hélicoptères ont pris la foudre.  — *A bolt snaps out of the cloud: two helicopters go black and smoking and spin gently down to a field, where their sooty crews climb out, hair on end, and salute.*
5. Au tribunal, le tonnerre a gardé le silence.  — *Courtroom: the cloud is squeezed into the dock, bulging against the ceiling, two small gendarmes beside it; its mouth is pressed tightly shut.*
6. C'était son droit.  — *Its box-headed lawyer lays a hand on the cloud and nods gravely at the judge.*
7. Ce mec n'en demandait pas plus.  — *Public benches: the hero in pyjamas and nightcap sleeps peacefully, smiling.*
8. Le juge a insisté.  — *The judge leans over his bench, bangs the gavel and points at the cloud.*
9. Il a répondu.  — *The cloud opens its mouth: white flash, a visible shockwave ring blasts across the room, the judge's wig and every paper fly, the hero jolts awake.*
10. Le quartier a perdu toutes ses vitres.  — *Exterior street: every window of every building around the courthouse pops out at once and drops onto the empty pavement.*
11. Les douze jurés sont sourds.  — *Jury box among the falling dust: twelve jurors sit perfectly still and dignified, hair blown straight back; one calmly turns a page.*
12. Trois experts ont confirmé : c'était fort.  — *Three experts in white coats hold up a sound-level meter whose needle has bent off the dial, and nod together.*
13. Il a été placé en cellule capitonnée.  — *Guards push the bulging cloud through the narrow door of an all-white padded cell. Card COUPABLE stamps.*
14. Dans la cellule, il y avait déjà un mime.  — *Inside the padded cell: in the corner, a box-headed mime in a striped shirt and white face mimes an invisible wall, then gives a small wave.*
15. Le mime a demandé son transfert.  — *At the door hatch, the mime silently points at the cloud, then mimes packing a suitcase and walking away, while a guard watches blankly.*
16. À voix haute.  — *Close on the hatch: the mime shouts, mouth enormous, and the guard's cap flies off.*
17. À sa sortie, le tonnerre n'avait pas d'adresse.  — *Prison gate by day: the cloud floats outside holding a tiny bundle; a clerk at the gate shows it an empty register and shrugs.*
18. Assigné à résidence chez la victime.  — *Two gendarmes walk the cloud, an electronic tag strapped to its bolt arm, up to the hero's front door; the hero opens it in pyjamas. Card ASSIGNÉ stamps.*
19. Il a pris le côté fenêtre.  — *Night, the hero's double bed: the cloud is tucked under the duvet on the window side, eyes open and calm; the hero lies on the door side, wide awake, staring at the ceiling. No rain, no snoring.*

Cartes : INTERPELLÉ (line 3), COUPABLE (line 13), ASSIGNÉ (line 18).

Gag récurrent : Silence: everyone who wants quiet loses it. The thunder gives the hero the silence he wanted at the trial (lines 5-7), then the court loses its windows, the mime breaks his silence to get away (lines 14-16), and the hero ends up with the noise in his own bed (line 19).

## Audit

- Corpus checked: all 77 stories7/*/lines.json (every published episode). No exact duplicate lines.
- Near-identical lines, all from « La table basse »: 'Condamné à perpétuité' vs 'Trois gardiens. Perpétuité.'; 'Son codétenu est un mime.' vs 'Sa codétenue, c'est la table.'; 'Au procès, le tonnerre a gardé le silence.' copies the opening and turn of 'Au procès, elle n'a exprimé aucun remords.' (the defendant's attitude in court); 'Libéré en appel' vs 'En appel, la table a passé une radio.'
- Spine: the second half partly re-skins « La table basse » (object arrested, trial with a line about how it behaves in court, prison sentence, appeal, then the hero living with the culprit). What differs: no repeat offence in prison, and the deaf jury, the experts, the mime cellmate and house arrest at the victim's home because it has no address are all new. Once the shared wording is removed (Au procès / perpétuité / codétenu / en appel), the remaining overlap is the trial-and-prison shape, which the founding template shares by design (the tornado is also sentenced). Acceptable.
- « L'écho »: same family (a sound treated as a person by the law), and its last line punishes 'tapage'. The original line 2 'plainte pour tapage nocturne' reused that word and that legal charge. The spines differ (the echo goes missing, a search is launched, it comes back late, the hero is fined), so only the wording needed fixing.
- « Le hoquet »: not close (a body symptom, then seismographs, an evacuation and a natural disaster declared). Nothing shared after the rewrite. « L'éternuement » (weather service, storm names) has no shared beat either.
- Staging risk, « La grève » and « Le parapluie »: both have clouds as characters, and in « Le parapluie » a small cloud follows the hero and ends up living with him. The thundercloud must read differently (big, black, anvil-shaped, angry face, bolt arms, shockwave rings) and must never rain: water on the ground is a banned premise.
- Fifteen most recent episodes: no shared image or turn with feuille, place, rambarde, barbecue, flaque, guepe, bocal, centime, ascenseur, parcmetre, conseiller, bus or robinet. « Le pull » ends on a life sentence ('À vie'), one more reason to drop 'perpétuité'. « La table basse » is the only real overlap.
- Line 17 'Faute d'adresse' can also be read as 'maladresse' (clumsiness), so it is a possible pun, which the rules ban. It also packs two ideas into one shot.
- Line 16 'Libéré en appel pour bonne conduite' gets the law wrong (release for good conduct is not decided on appeal) and is two images in one line.
- Deaf jury: allowed, because the jurors are not the butt of the joke. They are the only people in the room who are not affected, and the joke is on the court's logic. Deafness cannot be seen, though, so the line only reads if the jurors stay calm while the windows blow out. The staging must give them dignity.
- Production (judged 6.5): thunder is a sound with no body. It needs one character design used in every shot, and 'Il a répondu' needs a visible effect (flash plus shockwave ring). The trial half had no damage beside it (the only damage was the helicopters in line 4).
- Reserved premises: snoring is held back for another episode, so the final bed shot must not show the cloud rumbling in its sleep.
