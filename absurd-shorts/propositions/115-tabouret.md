# Histoire 115 — « Le tabouret »

Écrite par trois auteurs en parallèle (travail et bureau ; ville et voisinage ; loisirs et corps), départagée par trois juges (comédie, rétention, fabrication), corrigée selon leurs notes et vérifiée contre tous les épisodes publiés.

Prémisse : Dans son garage, ce mec fabrique un tabouret. Il est bancal. Sa femme glisse sans un mot un sous-bock plié sous le pied court ; vexé, ce mec refuse qu'on le cale, retire le sous-bock, le plaque sur l'assise et se rassoit dessus. Le sous-bock reste collé au fond de son pantalon pour toute la vidéo. Il scie le pied trop long, puis tous les autres : sans pieds, toujours bancal. Le problème, c'est donc le sol. Il rabote le parquet jusque dans la soupe du voisin du dessous, découvre que l'immeuble penche et le cale au cric, rabote la rue, puis la colline. Les montagnes, ce ne sont que des pieds trop longs : il les scie. Les copeaux font une montagne, il la rabote, et ça fait encore des copeaux. Le pays devient plat comme une table : les rivières s'arrêtent, faute de savoir de quel côté couler, les cyclistes du dimanche pleurent de joie (les skieurs, moins) et, depuis la capitale, on voit la mer des deux côtés. Ce mec se rassoit sur son tabouret : toujours bancal. Le problème, c'est la Terre : elle est ronde. Il la cale avec le sous-bock qu'il refusait au début, et qu'il avait aux fesses depuis.

## Texte final

1. Ce mec a fabriqué un tabouret bancal. Il a refusé qu'on le cale. — *Two cuts. Garage workshop (garageShed, open front, a workbench with a toolbox, a vice and a pegboard of tool silhouettes; no lettering on any tool): the hero, in his suit, sleeves rolled, sits proudly on a brand-new four-legged pine stool (new model: the engine only has the red metal barStool; four separate legs readable at phone size), and it rocks under him, tock, tock, one leg tapping the floor each time with a small dustPuff. Cut: his wife (new: round, floral apron, hair in a bun, deadpan, never speaks) leans into frame and, without a word, slides the sous-bock under the short leg (new: a rounded-corner square card, bright red with one off-centre cream wave running from corner to corner, no ring, no centred disc, no lettering, folded in two, scaled ×2 so it reads); the stool stops dead. The hero stares at her, offended, pulls the card out, slaps it flat on the seat, sits back down on it, and starts rocking again; when he stands up in the next line it is stuck to the seat of his trousers (running gag starts, never mentioned). Two people in shot.*
2. Il a scié le pied trop long. Puis tous les autres. — *One framing, side view at stool height, hard cuts on the beat: the hero, bent over with his back three-quarters to camera (the red card on his backside, bright against the dark suit, shown as a decal facing the lens), saws a slice off one leg with a handsaw (sawdust in opaque puffs); he sits, it rocks the other way, tock; he saws the next leg, then the next, then the last, each cut dropping the stool lower until his knees are up by his ears.*
3. Sans pieds, toujours bancal. Le problème, c'était le sol. — *Two cuts. The stool is now just its round seat lying flat on the concrete, four little stumps sawn flush; the hero sits on it cross-legged at floor level, and it still rocks, tock, tock. BANCAL card stamps. Cut: he slowly turns his head and glares down at the floor itself, eyes narrowed (Looney-Tunes squint, one eyebrow climbing), then points an accusing finger at it.*
4. Il a raboté le parquet. Jusque dans la soupe du voisin du dessous. — *Two cuts. His living room (livingRoom with a new carpet: false option, or a pale parquet plane laid over the rug with a polygon offset): the hero on his knees, back to camera (card on show), pushes an oversized carpenter's plane (new: a wooden block plane with a steel blade and a front knob, 40 cm long) along the boards; long opaque curly shavings fly up and pile round him like ribbons. Cut: two-flat cutaway (moCutaway from « Le mot », the beds swapped for a table and a soupBowl, pushed in until heads are head-sized): downstairs, the neighbour (vest, napkin tucked in his collar) eats soup; a rectangle of his ceiling opens like a lid and the hero's face and plane appear in the hole; shavings snow into the soup. The neighbour looks up, spoon frozen. Two people in shot.*
5. L'immeuble penchait. Il l'a calé au cric. — *Street outside his building: one tall stand-alone facade (new: a five-storey box with a window grid; cottageStreet only has single-storey cottages) leans a few degrees to the left like a tired man. The hero lies under the low corner and cranks a car jack (new: a red scissor jack at ×3 scale); the building creaks level in three jerks, and in a second-floor window a flowerpot slides back to the middle of the sill on its own. The card on his trousers faces camera as he works. One person in shot.*
6. La rue penchait. Il l'a rabotée. Puis la colline. — *Two cuts. The same street, visibly sloping downhill: the hero walks down it pushing the carpenter's plane scaled up to the size of a lawnmower, and the road surface rolls up behind him in one long grey asphalt ribbon (a curl mesh growing along a path), leaving the street dead flat; a parked car (car with plate: ' ') settles level with a little bounce. Cut: a green hill behind the village (a separate low cone on grassland): the hero, tiny, on top of it, planes the summit off; a green turf curl rolls down the slope like a carpet and the hill is left a low flat mesa. One person per cut.*
7. Les montagnes, c'étaient des pieds trop longs. Il les a sciées. — *Mountain range (mountainRange from « L'écho », with one separate cuttable peak: a cone with its snow cap split into a stump and a falling block; a new ski lift, a cable with three empty chairs): the hero stands at the foot of the tallest peak with the line 2 handsaw scaled ×20, its handle as big as he is, sawing side to side across the frame (never along the lens axis) with the same wrist motion as in line 2, back to camera, card on show; the peak slides off its stump in one block and lands flat with a whump and a big white dustPuff. Behind, two other peaks already stand as flat stumps.*
8. Les copeaux ont fait une montagne. Il l'a rabotée. Encore des copeaux. — *His small back garden: a huge cone of curls (a cone mesh with a curl texture and instanced curls on its surface only, in pine yellow, asphalt grey and snowy white) towers above the roof. The hero stands on top of a tall ladder (ladder) planing the top of the heap with the lawnmower-sized plane; the new shavings tumble down the sides and pile up again at the foot, the heap never shrinking. His wife hangs washing on a line (clothesline) in front of it, not looking. Two people in shot.*
9. Le pays est plat comme une table. — *High aerial shot over the countryside (cottageRows and grassland, wide): roofs, fields and roads lie on one perfectly flat plane like a tabletop, the sawn mountain stumps visibly flat on the horizon. In the foreground, on a village square, a single steel pétanque boule (boule) sits motionless; an old man in a cap nudges it with his foot; it does not roll an inch. PLAT card stamps. One person in shot.*
10. Les rivières se sont arrêtées. Elles ne savent plus de quel côté couler. — *Riverbank (river from « L'écho »): the river lies dead still like a strip of blue glass. A fat trout (fish recoloured, big cartoon eyes) pokes its head up in the middle, looks left, looks right, left again, and sinks back with a shrug. A paper boat sits on the water without moving; a fisherman in a bob hat pokes it with his rod (poFishingRod); nothing. One person in shot.*
11. Les cyclistes du dimanche pleurent de joie. Les skieurs, moins. — *Two cuts. An endless straight flat country road: three Sunday cyclists (bicycle) staggered on a diagonal, not side by side, in plain coloured jerseys (no numbers, no logos, no three-colour stripes), all three crying with joy, opaque tears flying back from their eyes (tearFountain). Cut: the sawn-off mountain, now a vast flat white plain: one skier in goggles pushes himself along with his poles, painfully, a centimetre at a time; above him the ski-lift chairs hang a metre off the flat snow. Three people at most.*
12. De la capitale, on voit la mer. Des deux côtés. — *Two cuts on a small balcony over the city roofs (balcony; parisStreet roofs with eiffel: false, no real monument in frame): the hero and his wife stand at the railing, coffee cups in hand. Cut one: both look left; past the rooftops the flat land runs off, and on the horizon a thick bright blue band of sea. Cut two: both turn their heads right at the same moment; on that horizon too, a thick bright blue band of sea. The wife sips. The card on his trousers shows as he turns. Two people in shot.*
13. Ce mec s'est rassis sur son tabouret. — *Back in the garage, same framing as line 1: the hero lowers himself with ceremony onto the legless round seat on the perfectly flat floor, closes his eyes, smiles, and settles; the red card on his backside is the last thing to touch the seat.*
14. Toujours bancal. — *Close, at floor level: the seat rocks, tock. Tock. The hero's eyes snap open. His wife, in the doorway behind him, raises one eyebrow.*
15. Le problème, c'était la Terre. Elle est ronde. — *Three cuts joined by a cloud wipe (cloudLayer): the garage roof from above; a high aerial over the flat country; then space (spaceDark, plain; not spaceVoid, which adds a ringed salmon planet): the low-poly Earth fills the frame (earthGlobe, continents generated from noise, no real map), its top flattened into one facet like a tabletop. The whole planet rocks gently side to side on a pivot, tock, tock, with exactly the tapping sound of the stool in lines 1 to 3. Hold until the rock reads.*
16. Il l'a calée avec un sous-bock. — *Two cuts, at cartoon scale. Space: the hero, as big as a continent, floats beside the Earth in his suit with a round goldfish-bowl helmet (spaceHelmet), reaches round, peels the folded red card off the seat of his trousers with a little sticky sound, and slides it under the bottom of the planet like under a table leg; the card is a fifth of the Earth's diameter, bright red against the plain dark. The Earth stops rocking. Cut in to the flat facet on top: the hero sits on his legless stool, arms crossed, satisfied, and does not rock. Hold one beat, deadpan.*

Cartes : BANCAL (line 3), PLAT (line 9).

Gag récurrent : The sous-bock. In line 1 the hero's wife fixes the wobbly stool in one second with a folded red card; the hero, offended, pulls it out, slaps it on the seat and sits on it, and from then on it is stuck to the seat of his dark trousers in every shot where he is seen from the side or from behind (2, 4, 5, 7, 12, 13), bright red, oversized, never mentioned. He saws legs, planes the floor, the street, the hill and the mountains with the fix on his own backside. The careful viewer knows long before line 16 that the answer to the whole story is the cheapest object in it, and that it is going to come off his trousers; what they cannot guess is where it will go. The text never names it until the last line. By ear, line 1 now says the refusal out loud (« Il a refusé qu'on le cale »), and the verb comes back twice: he wedges a whole building (« Il l'a calé au cric », 5) and finally the planet with the very wedge he refused (« Il l'a calée avec un sous-bock », 16). « Bancal » (1) comes back as « toujours bancal » (3, 14); « Le problème, c'était le sol » (3) comes back as « Le problème, c'était la Terre » (15); « le pied trop long » (2) comes back as « des pieds trop longs » (7), which is the logic of the whole escalation (each fix reveals the next thing that leans: the stool, the floor, the building, the street, the hill, the mountains, the planet); « plat comme une table » (9) brings the country back to furniture. The wife never speaks; she appears in lines 1, 8, 12 and 14, always calm, always right: the butt is the husband's pride, not her. The ending is a resolution at planetary scale, not a loop, and nobody is guilty: the Earth is simply round.

## Notes du jury

- Comédie : 8,5/10. Appliqué : L4 du juge, « Il a raboté le parquet. Jusque dans la soupe du voisin du dessous. », qui donne une blague à la ligne au lieu d'un lieu (et éloigne « La fuite est ressortie chez le voisin du dessous ») ; L8 du juge, « Les copeaux ont fait une montagne. Il l'a rabotée. Encore des copeaux. », seule boucle entendue de l'histoire, le jardin montré et non dit ; L5 et L6 gardées en deux respirations rapides ; L7 et L9 à L16 mot pour mot ; « sous-bock » jamais dit avant la L16 ; la Terre bascule avec exactement le « toc » du tabouret. Non appliqué : L1 à L3 mot pour mot, parce que la consigne de finition demande une accroche plus neuve et que la rétention signale le piège « plus » ; la fierté du bricoleur, que le juge voyait dans l'image de la L1, est maintenant dite (« Il a refusé qu'on le cale ») et donne le verbe de la chute. Vérifié contre le corpus : « Le rayon » (le parasol au garage, que l'auteur citait sous le nom « Le soleil » : ici la solution bon marché est appliquée, pas regrettée), « La tondeuse » (un raisonnement, pas une machine emballée, et la fin quitte la planète), « Le bocal » (la femme résout tout en L1 et se fait refuser), « La bosse » (« Les skieurs sont tombés de haut » : écho léger, gardé), « Le robinet » et « L'aimant » (la carte redessinée ; aplatir le pays est neuf) ; aucun « X vaut Y », aucune loi, aucun expert, aucun pays qui imite. Fin hors du sol : la seule de cette sortie.
- Rétention : 7,5/10 (8,5 corrigée). Appliqué : accroche plus neuve dès les premières secondes : la L1 dit le refus de la cale (« Ce mec a fabriqué un tabouret bancal. Il a refusé qu'on le cale. »), une question de caractère posée vers 2 s, et le mot « cale » planté pour la dernière ligne ; L2 raccourcie à la version du juge, « Il a scié le pied trop long. Puis tous les autres. », si bien que le sol arrive vers 6 s au lieu de 8 ; L3 sans le piège de la voix de synthèse sur « plus » (« Plus de pieds » pouvait s'entendre « encore des pieds »), version du juge : « Sans pieds, toujours bancal. Le problème, c'était le sol. », qui prépare à l'oreille « Le problème, c'était la Terre » ; L10 gardée ; L15 et L16 mot pour mot ; sous-bock grand et rouge vif sur le costume sombre dans chaque plan de dos. Non appliqué : L8 « Les copeaux ont fait une montagne. Elle penchait. », remplacée par la version comédie, qui corrige aussi le « Il l'a rabotée aussi » qui sonnait comme un problème réglé (« Encore des copeaux » le relance). 148 mots, 16 lignes, environ 41 s.
- Fabrication : 7/10.
