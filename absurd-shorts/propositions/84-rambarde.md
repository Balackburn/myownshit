# Histoire 84 — « La rambarde »

Écrite par la boucle d'écriture : cinq nouveaux auteurs (police, armée, justice, secours et État, escalade totale), plus « La bougie », deuxième de l'histoire 83, remise en compétition. Jury de trois (comédie, rétention, fabrication), puis réécriture par l'auteur principal. Interdits pour cet épisode, parce que « La flaque » et « Le barbecue » les ont utilisés : sous-marin, torpille, « trois cents X », ville déplacée, alerte nucléaire, masques à gaz, parachutistes, lance-flammes, quarantaine, échange de prisonniers, espion, fête nationale de la nuisance, ministre qui la goûte, caserne détruite, quartier incendié, dernière phrase en « Ah, et… », coupable qui était autre chose, héros qui refait la même erreur, cessez-le-feu, médailles, char à travers une façade.

## Texte final

1. Ce mec s'est coincé la tête dans la rambarde.  — *Tight shot against open sky: a box head jammed sideways between two balcony railing bars, cheeks squashed, arms dangling over the rail. No ground is in frame.*
2. Les pompiers ont sorti la grande échelle.  — *A fire engine screeches up and its ladder telescopes up and up until the tip vanishes into the clouds.*
3. Il habite au rez-de-chaussée.  — *Wide reveal: the balcony sits at pavement level, his slippers a few centimetres off the ground and a metre from the firemen, while the giant ladder towers uselessly into the sky.*
4. Douze pompiers ont tiré sur ses jambes.  — *Twelve firemen in a row grab his legs, lift him horizontal and heave backwards like a tug of war, with the ladder still raised into the clouds behind.*
5. Il a pris vingt centimètres.  — *His body stretches like elastic while his head stays stuck, and all twelve firemen fall flat on their backs. From here on he is visibly twenty centimetres taller in every shot.*
6. La rambarde a été déclarée catastrophe naturelle.  — *A minister in a white hard hat leans from a podium on the pavement and slams a giant rubber stamp onto the railing, which is already wrapped in red-and-white tape, the hero's head included.*
7. Le président a ordonné à la tête de sortir.  — *Nose to nose with the trapped head, the president in his sash bellows into a megaphone. The blast makes the railing bars quiver like a tuning fork.*
8. La tête est restée sur ses positions.  — *Tight close-up: the bars stop quivering, the box head has not moved a millimetre and stares back deadpan, while beside it the president's megaphone slowly droops.*
9. Le quartier a été évacué par hélicoptère.  — *Helicopters with searchlights winch residents off every roof and balcony of the street on cables, one still sitting in her armchair. The fire ladder is still in the clouds.*
10. Sauf lui.  — *The empty street at dusk: he stands alone with his head in the railing as the last helicopter flies off overhead, the giant ladder still raised beside him.*
11. Les démineurs ont fait sauter l'immeuble.  — *Behind a line of riot police with shields, two bomb-squad officers in padded suits push down a detonator, and the whole building goes up in a giant fireball.*
12. Autour de la rambarde.  — *The dust clears on a crater where only the blackened railing still stands, with the hero still in it, his box head sooty and a thin wisp of smoke rising, the ladder still raised behind. The RÉUSSI card stamps only once the dust has settled.*
13. L'immeuble a eu des funérailles nationales.  — *Soldiers with rifles slow-march a single brick on a velvet cushion under a draped flag, a military band playing behind, the fire ladder towering over the procession.*
14. La rambarde a été classée monument historique.  — *On the crater's rim an official cuts a ribbon and unveils a bronze plaque beside the lone railing, with the hero still stuck in it, while a soldier salutes.*
15. Tête comprise.  — *The bronze plaque fills half the frame: a huge, thick engraved arrow points straight at his head.*
16. Il a penché la tête. Elle est sortie.  — *He calmly tilts his head a quarter turn and it pops out between the bars in one go. He blinks, free, twenty centimetres too tall.*
17. Il a été arrêté pour vol.  — *Masked special forces drop on ropes from a helicopter and pin him face down on the rubble beside the empty railing, with the ladder still in the clouds.*
18. Sa tête a été saisie.  — *In an interrogation room under a hanging lamp, a detective wraps red-and-white seal tape round the hero's box head (his face still showing) and presses a big red wax seal onto his forehead with a thump; the hero stays deadpan.*
19. Il loue sa tête à l'État.  — *At a government counter, stooping because he is twenty centimetres too tall for the window, he slides a banknote under the glass and the clerk brings a big round official seal down on his forehead, leaving an ink ring. Through the window behind, the fire ladder still rises into the clouds.*

Cartons : RÉUSSI — ligne 12 (Autour de la rambarde.), une fois la poussière retombée ; CLASSÉE — ligne 14 (La rambarde a été classée monument historique.) ; ARRÊTÉ — ligne 17 (Il a été arrêté pour vol.)

## Retouches après la réécriture (éditeur)

- Ligne 8 : « La tête a refusé d'obtempérer. » devient « La tête est restée sur ses positions. ». « La table basse » (80) disait déjà « La table a refusé d'obtempérer. ».
- Ligne 12 : « Par précaution. » devient « Autour de la rambarde. ». « La guêpe » (81) disait déjà « Et la mairie, par précaution. ». La nouvelle phrase dit l'absurde par le détail : on fait sauter l'immeuble autour de la tête coincée.
- Ligne 18 : « Et pour l'immeuble. » devient « Sa tête a été saisie. ». « La table basse » finissait déjà sur « condamné pour coups et blessures. / Et pour les trois immeubles. ». La saisie prépare aussi la dernière phrase : l'État a saisi sa tête, il la lui loue.

## Changements apportés par la réécriture

- Line 1 is now framed tight on the head and bars against the sky, with no ground in frame, so that « Il habite au rez-de-chaussée. » works as a real reveal (retention judge).
- Line 6 is trimmed to « La rambarde a été déclarée catastrophe naturelle. » (7 words). The in-image « CATASTROPHE NATURELLE » sign is gone because text inside the image does not read on a phone. The minister now does something physical: he stamps the taped railing from his podium (all three judges).
- Line 7 is now physical: the megaphone blast makes the bars quiver like a tuning fork instead of showing a man talking (fabrication judge).
- Line 8 is sharpened to « La tête a refusé d'obtempérer. », which uses police-report jargon. The static close-up now has a gag: the bars go still and the president's megaphone droops (retention and fabrication judges).
- Lines 16 and 17 are merged into one shot: « Il a penché la tête. Elle est sortie. » The release now takes a single beat, because its triviality is the joke. « sortie » echoes the president's order to « sortir », and the arrest comes one cut later. The flat line « Elle est sortie toute seule. » is gone (all three judges).
- In the freed slot I added the topper « Et pour l'immeuble. » right after the arrest. I took the move from Le coq, where the collateral damage is charged to the wrong party: here the hero pays for the State's « par précaution ». The image is a detective slapping the crater photo onto his case file. The script stays at 19 lines (comedy judge).
- The last line drops « Depuis, » and becomes « Il loue sa tête à l'État. » (6 words, the driest line in the script). The ear tag « PROPRIÉTÉ DE L'ÉTAT », too small to read, is replaced by a big round official seal stamped on his forehead as he pays. The same shot pays off both running gags: he stoops because he is twenty centimetres too tall for the window, and the ladder is still in the clouds outside (all three judges). I did not use « condamné à la remettre », which ends with the hero back where he started.
- Title cards: I dropped REFUSÉ, which would now repeat « refusé d'obtempérer » word for word, kept RÉUSSI and CLASSÉE, and put ARRÊTÉ on the twist. This follows the comedy judge. The retention judge wanted to keep REFUSÉ; I chose to give the reversal the stamp instead.
- Line 12: box-headed characters have no hair, so 'hair smoking' is now a sooty box head with a wisp of smoke. RÉUSSI stamps after the dust clears so it does not hide the railing standing alone in the crater (fabrication and retention judges).
- Line 15: the plaque now fills half the frame and the engraved arrow is thick, so it reads at phone size (fabrication judge).
- More police and army, as the client asked, without adding lines: riot police with shields cordon the demolition (11), soldiers with rifles and a military band carry the brick (13), a soldier salutes the plaque (14), and special forces drop from a helicopter for the arrest (17).
- The fire ladder stays raised into the clouds in the background of every shot from line 2 to the end, and the twenty-centimetre stretch stays visible from line 5 on. Both running gags cost no words.
- The ladder and ground-floor beat is kept because it was cleared for use. Candidate 6 is not produced alongside this one.
- Rule check: 19 lines, each 2 to 9 words; line 1 starts with « Ce mec »; numbers in words (douze, vingt); French only; no real names; no exclamation marks. Nothing from the banned list is used: no « Ah, et … » ending, no medals, no ceasefire, no town carried away, no hero repeating the first mistake. Estimated narration is about 32 to 34 s plus card beats.

## Second : « Le coq »

A man woken at dawn by a rooster files a noise complaint; the police arrest the church weathercock, which is tried and jailed, and when the real rooster crows again the man is arrested for disturbing the peace while the rooster moves into his flat under police protection.

1. Ce mec était réveillé par un coq.
2. Il a porté plainte pour tapage nocturne.
3. Deux cents policiers ont bouclé le village.
4. Le suspect a été repéré sur le clocher.
5. Il n'a opposé aucune résistance.
6. Le clocher est tombé sur la mairie.
7. Le suspect a aussi été inculpé pour la mairie.
8. Ce mec l'a formellement identifié.
9. Son silence a été retenu contre lui.
10. Au procès, il a tourné le dos au juge.
11. Vingt ans pour outrage.
12. Il purge sa peine sur le mirador.
13. À cinq heures, le coq a récidivé.
14. Ce mec a réveillé la police.
15. Il a été arrêté pour tapage nocturne.
16. Le coq a témoigné contre lui.
17. Il a été placé sous protection policière.
18. La police l'a relogé chez ce mec.
19. Le coq dort jusqu'à midi.

## Autres candidats

- La haie (armee) : A man complains that his neighbour's hedge pokes ten centimetres over the fence; the army treats it as an invasion and digs a front line through his living room, and a summit of world leaders makes the trench the official border, which leaves his own toilets in another country.
- Le cactus (justice) : A man pricks his finger on his cactus and files a complaint for attempted murder, so the State arrests, tries, disarms and jails the cactus with complete seriousness, until the appeal court acquits it and makes the man pay it a pension.
- Le train (total) : A man misses his train by four seconds and reports it for hit-and-run, so the police, then the army, then the courts hunt the train down, arrest it and give it a life sentence.
- La bougie (retour) : A man can't blow out his birthday candle, so the emergency services and the State throw their whole crisis plan at it, and wreck a district doing it, until the State makes it the eternal flame and it goes out the next morning.
