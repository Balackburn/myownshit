# Histoire 82 — « La flaque »

Écrite par la boucle d'écriture (cinq auteurs, un par angle : police, armée, justice, secours et État, escalade totale ; jury de trois : comédie, rétention, fabrication), puis réécrite par l'auteur principal. Interdits pour cet épisode, parce que les deux précédents les ont utilisés : le char à travers une façade, le rasage « par précaution », le cessez-le-feu, les médailles, le procès sans remords, le héros condamné à la fin, le coupable qui était une autre chose.

## Jury

- La flaque (armee) : 8, 8.5, 7 ; moyenne 7.8 ; 2 voix
- « Le détecteur » (secours) : 7.5, 7.5, 8.5 ; moyenne 7.8 ; 1 voix
- L'odeur (police) : 7, 7, 7.5 ; moyenne 7.2 ; 0 voix
- « Le fromage » (justice) : 7, 8, 6 ; moyenne 7.0 ; 0 voix
- Le cheveu (total) : 6.5, 7.5, 6.5 ; moyenne 6.8 ; 0 voix

## Texte final

1. Ce mec a marché dans une flaque.  — *A box-headed man in a suit, briefcase in hand, steps into a small puddle on a quiet sunny street and water splashes up one trouser leg; his face does not change. Low side camera, puddle in the foreground.*
2. Il a signalé une inondation.  — *The man, one trouser leg soaked and dripping, holds a phone to his ear and points straight down at the doormat-sized puddle at his feet.*
3. La marine l'a déclarée eaux ennemies.  — *In a navy office, an officer in an anchor cap slams a red stamp onto a city map where the tiny puddle is circled in red, and the ENNEMIE card stamps down with it.*
4. Un sous-marin a plongé dedans.  — *A full-size grey submarine, tilted nose down above the street, plunges into the puddle and vanishes completely, tail fin last, while the puddle keeps its doormat size and barely ripples. No helicopter.*
5. Il est remonté avec la rue sur le dos.  — *One block away the asphalt bulges and splits, and the submarine rises through it with a slab of street on its deck. Two parked cars and a streetlamp slowly slide off its back.*
6. Sa torpille a traversé la boulangerie.  — *The submarine swivels toward the puddle and fires; the torpedo skims over the puddle and punches through the bakery, baguettes spinning out of every window.*
7. Et la caserne des pompiers.  — *The torpedo bursts out of the back wall of the fire station and a red fire engine somersaults through the air, ladder fully extended.*
8. La flaque n'a rien eu.  — *Smoke and rubble all around; in the middle, the puddle lies perfectly still, mirroring the blue sky. In the background the man wrings out his wet sock. INTACTE card.*
9. L'aviation a promis une frappe chirurgicale.  — *In the press room an air force general at the podium holds up one finger and taps a map where a single tiny crosshair sits on the puddle.*
10. Trois cents bombes.  — *Three fighter jets in tight formation release a dense carpet of bombs that falls over the street in neat rows.*
11. Trois cents cratères.  — *Locked top-down aerial: the whole neighbourhood is pocked with dry craters in perfect rows, and only the man's original little puddle shines in one corner. The frame stays locked for the next two shots.*
12. Puis il a plu.  — *Same locked aerial: one tiny grey cloud, alone in an empty blue sky, drifts in and rains over the craters. No card, no sound effect on top.*
13. Trois cents flaques.  — *Same locked aerial: every crater is now a glittering puddle reflecting the sky. Hold a full second with nothing stepping on it.*
14. Le ministre a commandé trois cents sous-marins.  — *At the podium the minister signs an order form so long that it unrolls off the podium, across the press-room floor and out the door.*
15. Un par flaque.  — *Low wide shot: three hundred periscopes pop up at the same instant, one from every puddle. In the foreground the man stands beside his original little puddle, the only one with no periscope.*
16. Avec la ville sur le dos.  — *All the hulls break the surface together in one wave of spray and the whole town rises three metres on their backs like a lid. Only a small round patch of old street stays below, with the man and his puddle on it.*
17. Ils l'ont garée au port.  — *The whole town floats at a harbour quay on its submarines, a thick mooring rope looped round the town hall clock tower, while a gendarme tucks a parking ticket under the town hall door.*
18. Officiellement, la ville a déménagé.  — *At the podium the minister slides the town's icon across a big wall map from inland to the coast with one finger, and the DÉMÉNAGÉE card stamps down.*
19. Ce mec, non.  — *Wide high shot: the man stands alone with his briefcase on his little round patch of pavement, in the middle of an empty bare plain where the town used to be.*
20. La flaque non plus.  — *A frame-for-frame copy of shot 1, with the same low side angle: he takes one step and his shoe lands in the same little puddle, water splashing up the same trouser leg, face unchanged. Cut on the splash, on the last syllable, so the video loops into its hook.*

Cartons : ENNEMIE — ligne 3 (La marine l'a déclarée eaux ennemies.) ; INTACTE — ligne 8 (La flaque n'a rien eu.) ; DÉMÉNAGÉE — ligne 18 (Officiellement, la ville a déménagé.)

## Changements apportés par la réécriture

- Inserted a new line 2, « Il a signalé une inondation. », to give the hero his petty, overstated complaint. The navy now answers him instead of acting on its own (both comedy and retention). I avoided « porter plainte » and « exiger », which La guêpe already used.
- Merged old lines 3-4 into « Un sous-marin a plongé dedans. » The sub dives nose first and vanishes into a doormat-sized puddle, with no helicopter. The gag is now 'the puddle is bottomless', which moves away from La guêpe's carrier in the sink.
- Old line 5 became « Il est remonté avec la rue sur le dos. » (9 words). « Il a fait surface avec la rue sur le dos » runs to 10 words, and « ressorti » would echo La table basse. This keeps the « avec la … sur le dos » pattern that line 16 calls back.
- Split the plot-only line « L'aviation a largué trois cents bombes. » into « L'aviation a promis une frappe chirurgicale. / Trois cents bombes. » The institution's promise of precision is undercut by the carpet bombing, stated as a fragment. This uses the spin logic the panel praised in candidate 4 in a form native to this story. 'Trois cents' still appears exactly four times (bombes, cratères, flaques, sous-marins).
- Cut old line 15 « Ils ont fait surface ensemble. », flagged flat by all three judges. Its image is folded into « Avec la ville sur le dos. », where all the hulls surface at once under the town.
- Closed the logic gap and planted the ending without words. In the locked aerial, the original puddle is the only one already shining among the dry craters. In « Un par flaque. » it is the only puddle without a periscope: the minister ordered three hundred for the three hundred new ones and forgot the first. The town then lifts off everywhere except the man's small round patch, so « Ce mec, non. / La flaque non plus. » follows logically.
- Protected the multiplication run. One locked aerial camera covers « Trois cents cratères. / Puis il a plu. / Trois cents flaques. », with a tiny lone cloud, a full-second hold on the glittering puddles, and no card or sound effect on those lines.
- Kept the hero visible in the middle stretch: he wrings out his sock behind the intact puddle (line 8) and stands by his puddle in the foreground when the periscopes appear (line 15).
- Made line 14's image physical: an order form unrolling out of the press room replaces the submarine chart, which would not read on a phone.
- Kept « Ils l'ont garée au port. » and made it a clear sight gag: a mooring rope around the town hall clock tower and a gendarme leaving a parking ticket. I dropped the warships we do not have, so only the sub hulls show.
- Kept « Officiellement, la ville a déménagé. » word for word, with the minister sliding the town icon, as the paperwork covering the blunder. I left out the on-screen 'Bilan : zéro flaque' chart because the DÉMÉNAGÉE stamp already gives that shot its single text element.
- Kept « La flaque non plus. » as the driest last line and rejected all three alternatives. The last shot is a frame-for-frame copy of shot 1 and cuts on the splash, so the video loops into its hook.
- Rule check: 20 lines; each is 3 to 9 words; French only; no real names; numbers in words; line 1 starts with « Ce mec »; last line is 4 words; no exclamation marks. Estimated spoken length is about 29 s, or about 34 s with cuts and the one-second hold. No banned beats: no tank, no razing « par précaution », no surrender or ceasefire, no medals, no trial, no conviction, no culprit swap.

## Autres candidats

- L'odeur (police) : A man finds a bad smell in his fridge and reports an intrusion; the police hunt the smell across the country, arrest his whole floor by mistake and question a cheese until it melts, then find the smell has lived in his flat long enough to be legally at home, so the police evict him instead.
- « Le fromage » (justice) : A man thinks his cheese stinks and files a complaint for assault, so the justice system arrests, tries and jails the cheese with complete seriousness. The smell empties every institution it gets near, and on appeal the model prisoner is placed under house arrest in the plaintiff's own flat.
- Le cheveu (total) : A man finds a hair in his soup. The police, the army and the courts each go after the hair with everything they have: they flatten a street, give the hair a life sentence and lose it through the bars, while the bald chef they arrested first stays in prison.
- « Le détecteur » (secours) : A man's smoke detector beeps for a low battery, so the firemen, finding no fire, light one to stay within protocol. The State's full emergency plan then burns and floods the whole neighbourhood, and the detector is the only thing left untouched, until the bomb squad kills the battery, which gets a state funeral and keeps beeping from the grave.
